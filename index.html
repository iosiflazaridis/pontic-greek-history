756 BC to 1923

The Pontic Greeks and their land

For 2,700 years Greeks lived on the southern shore of the Black Sea, from the founding of Trapezous by Sinope (by tradition in 756 BC) to the exchange of populations in 1923. This atlas maps their towns and villages as they were before 1923 and the peoples who lived beside them, and tells their history in twelve chapters.

Work in progress. A work by Iosif Lazaridis (@iosif_lazaridis on X), supervising research by Anthropic's Claude, from public sources and from sources kindly provided by readers. The atlas will be corrected and extended as feedback comes in; see About for how to send corrections and sources.
The core of the atlas

The six metropolises, c. 1920

Places: the Orthodox villages, towns and hamlets of each metropolis in the atlas's village list; "located" means an exact or approximate position. Communities, churches and schools: Black Book of the Central Council of Pontus (Athens 1922, p. 19), a Greek source. Its churches column adds up to 1,174, not the 1,134 printed.

How sources are handled

Source policy

  1. Every source is named.
  2. On contested topics the atlas gives the range of opinion, with each holder's origin: Greek, Greek diaspora, Turkish (mainstream or critical), Turkish-born scholar abroad, Armenian, international scholar, or contemporary witness.
  3. It says what was not found as well as what was, so that the gaps can be filled later.
  4. Possible duplicate villages are flagged, never merged without a source.

Greek villages before 1923

Every Orthodox village, town and hamlet found so far in the six metropolises, with the outer layer (Kars and Ardahan, Bayburt and the Sivas side) and the Greek-speaking Muslim villages. Hollow dots are approximate positions; places known only to their district are not drawn. Select a place to see its names, figures, fate, sources and what is still missing. About

A work in progress

2,700 Years of Pontic Greek History is a work by Iosif Lazaridis (@iosif_lazaridis on X), supervising research by Anthropic's Claude. It draws on public sources and on sources kindly provided by readers.

It is unfinished. Every figure and claim names its source, with the source's origin, and every village record says what is still missing.

Corrections and sources are welcome. To send a correction, a missing village or a document, write to @iosif_lazaridis on X.

Data and map credits

  • Elevation: Terrain Tiles on AWS (Mapzen; SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1). Coast, rivers, lakes and borders: Natural Earth (public domain).
  • Village coordinates and old names: mostly S. Nişanyan, Index Anatolicus (nisanyanyeradlari.com), with GeoNames and the pontosworld village maps.
  • Village lists: the Center for Asia Minor Studies list (via Samouilidis 1992 and pontosworld.com); Chrysanthos's 1913–14 table; the Greek consular census of 1911 as printed by Kitromilides & Alexandris (1984); the Black Book (1922).
  • Medieval monuments: A. Bryer & D. Winfield, The Byzantine Monuments and Topography of the Pontos (Dumbarton Oaks, 1985); crypto-Christian villages of 1857: A. Bryer, Δελτίο ΚΜΣ 4 (1983).
  • Every other source is listed, with its origin, under Sources.
